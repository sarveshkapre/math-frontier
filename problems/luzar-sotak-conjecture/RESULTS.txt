LUZAR–SOTAK RESEARCH — SECOND ITERATION
Date: 2026-11-26

STATUS
======
The general conjecture is not solved. No counterexample was found.
This iteration derived and independently checked an exact classification for
the infinite family of generalized Petersen graphs GP(n,2), and proved that
our previously proposed ten-vertex gadget is removable from a smallest
counterexample. Novelty of these derived results has NOT been established.

THE INFINITE-FAMILY RESULT
=========================
For n >= 5, GP(n,2) has vertices u_i,v_i (indices modulo n), and edges
u_i u_(i+1), u_i v_i, v_i v_(i+2). It is a simple cubic graph on 2n vertices.
The minimum number of colors in a proper 2-homogeneous coloring is:

  3 if 3 divides n;
  no valid coloring with any palette if n = 7;
  4 for every remaining n >= 5.

A proper 2-homogeneous coloring assigns different colors to adjacent vertices
and exactly two distinct colors to each vertex's three neighbors.

Proof components
----------------
1. Explicit upper bounds. For multiples of three, repeat outer word 012 and
   inner word 120. For n=8+3t, use outer 01021012 followed by t copies of 012,
   and inner 12210320 followed by t copies of 120. For n=10+3t, use outer
   0102302312 followed by t copies of 012 and inner 1220033120 followed by
   t copies of 120. The n=5 seed is outer 01012, inner 12300. A written local
   insertion proof shows that every repetition preserves all constraints.
   This is a proof for all n in the family, not extrapolation from samples.

2. Three-color necessity. A three-coloring corresponds exactly to a closed
   walk of length n in a transfer graph of 108 states and 180 transitions.
   A checked rank/phase certificate ensures every closed walk has length
   divisible by three. The verifier reconstructs every state and transition;
   it uses no graph library or SAT solver.

3. The n=7 exception. Its 14 vertices admit exactly 322 possible color classes
   that are independent and contain no complete neighborhood. A coloring with
   any number of colors is equivalent to a particular exact-cover problem on
   28 elements. A checked proof DAG with 778 states and 822 transitions proves
   this exact-cover problem infeasible. The verifier uses only Python's
   standard library. Independent SAT and equality-pattern searches agree.

THE GADGET CORRECTION
====================
Our earlier recommendation to seek a four-colorable complementary two-port
gadget forcing four distinct boundary colors was misguided.

In any four-coloring of such a fragment, each outside leaf has two allowed
colors once internal colors are fixed. Either one can match the opposite
attachment vertex, or both can match each other. Hence the four boundary
entries can always be made to use at most three distinct colors. The same
repetition conclusion holds with a five-color palette.

The ten-vertex fragment's conditional fifth-color obstruction is valid, but
it cannot obtain its necessary boundary condition from an independently
four-colorable complementary two-pole. More strongly, replacing this fragment
by the six-vertex K3,3-minus-an-edge fragment preserves both arbitrary-palette
feasibility and four-color feasibility, while removing four vertices.
A minimum-order counterexample therefore cannot contain the ten-vertex
fragment attached to the rest of the graph through exactly its two ports.

This does not rule out all counterexamples, all two-edge cuts, or other
gadgets. Full hypotheses, proof, witnesses, and edge cases are in
theory/two_pole_reduction.md and literature_review/reviewer_report.txt.

COMPLETED SEARCHES THIS ITERATION
================================
7,000 random/mutational graph instances: 1,000 at each order 18,22,26,30,34,40,50.
  Each is simple, connected, cubic, bridgeless, triangle-free, nonbipartite.
  All admit four-color witnesses. No node limit was reached.

159 generalized Petersen parameter pairs through 62 vertices, filtered for
  triangle-free nonbipartite graphs. 157 admit four-color witnesses. The two
  exceptions are isomorphic representations GP(7,2) and GP(7,3), which admit
  no valid coloring at all and are not counterexamples.

308 local perturbations of GP(7,2): 168 retained edge-subdivision-and-join
  outcomes and 140 retained edge-switch outcomes. All admit four colors.

1,333 solver-guided instances on 20 vertices. A SAT master chose triangle-free
  cubic graphs with a planted five-color witness, with five color classes of
  size four. A second SAT solver found a four-coloring of every candidate.
  Each such coloring was fed back to exclude every graph that it would color.
  Disconnected/bridged candidates received cut constraints. Of the 1,333
  completed instances, 1,307 were nonbipartite and 26 bipartite. The run stopped
  at its time budget (checked between solver calls; elapsed 144.339 seconds).
  This is a restricted, bounded synthesis run, not an exhaustive census.

Total: 8,800 graph instances in these batches; 8,798 checked four-color
witnesses, plus two representations of the known uncolorable exception.
Instances were not deduplicated up to graph isomorphism; these totals are NOT
counts of distinct isomorphism classes. Generic larger samples mostly colored
easily. The structural analysis produced the more useful result.

Experimental sources, seeds, data and verification summaries are under
alternative/ and synthesis/. The earlier archive's files are retained unchanged
under previous/; those earlier results are not counted as new work.

REPRODUCING THE THEOREMS
======================
From this directory, run:

  python3 verify_theorems.py

This needs Python 3.10+ and only its standard library. It checks the two-pole
tables/local cases, all construction seeds and seam invariants, the complete
three-color transfer certificate, and the unrestricted GP(7,2) certificate.
It also runs the independent review implementation. Written arguments explain
why these finite certificates prove the respective statements for all n.

To print a construction for any allowed n, for example 100:

  python3 theory/gp_n_2_construction.py 100

The broader experiments require g++, networkx and python-sat; reproduction
commands and versions are documented in alternative/experiment_notes.txt and
synthesis/README.txt. Compiled binaries are intentionally not included.

LITERATURE AND CREDIT
====================
The underlying question is Lužar and Soták's Problem 5.1 in:
  Barát et al., Open problems of the 33rd Workshop on Cycles and Colourings,
  arXiv:2511.02892 (2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02892v1

Hao, Liu and Romaniello, On 2-Homogeneous Colourings of Cubic Bipartite Graphs,
arXiv:2611.16649, submitted November 12, 2026, proves the cubic bipartite case;
Section 4 explicitly leaves the general cubic question open. Its Lemma 3.8
already gives a color-preserving edge-splicing lemma and its proof uses
prescribed-boundary Heawood three-poles. We do not claim those methods as new.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16649
  Readable manuscript mirror: https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2611.16649

Janicová, Madaras, Soták and Lužar, From NMNR-coloring of hypergraphs to
homogenous coloring of graphs, Ars Mathematica Contemporanea 12 (2017),
351–360, reports checking cubic BIPARTITE graphs through 26 vertices, p.358.
That historical cutoff does not apply to general cubic graphs, and the recent
bipartite theorem now subsumes it. No general-cubic published cutoff was found.

The focused literature check did not locate the exact GP(n,2) classification.
Related perfect/equitable and dynamic coloring papers concern different
conditions and do not by themselves establish this result. Absence of a search
match does not establish originality; see the detailed notes in
literature_review/generalized_petersen_literature.txt.

RECOMMENDED NEXT RESEARCH TARGET
================================
Check priority of the exact family theorem before presenting it as a new
discovery. Mathematically, try to extend the construction/certificate approach
to GP(n,k), starting with k=3 and then seeking statements uniform in k.
The long-term objective remains the general cubic conjecture. The current
proofs provide a working constructive method and a rigorous way to exclude
unproductive gadget constructions; they do not justify a claimed probability
or timetable for solving the general problem.
