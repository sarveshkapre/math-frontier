FOCUSED LITERATURE CHECK: 2-HOMOGENEOUS COLORINGS OF GP(n,2)
Date: 2026-11-26

Question checked: whether the statement that GP(n,2) admits a proper
2-homogeneous coloring with at most four colors for every n >= 5 except n = 7
is already present in accessible primary literature.

Result: no matching theorem was located in this search. This is not evidence
of priority or a guarantee that the result is new. In particular, unpublished
work and theses not indexed under the searched terminology remain a gap.

PRIMARY MATERIAL INSPECTED

1. Janicova, Madaras, Sotak, Luzar (2017), From NMNR-coloring of hypergraphs to
   homogenous coloring of graphs, Ars Mathematica Contemporanea 12, 351-360.
   Author-uploaded full text:
   https://www.researchgate.net/publication/313129431_From_NMNR-coloring_of_hypergraphs_to_homogenous_coloring_of_graphs
   The accessible full text has no occurrence of 'Petersen'. It proves the
   original six-color bipartite result and reports bipartite computational
   verification of the four-color conjecture through order 26, p. 358.

2. Luzar and Sotak, Section 5 of the 2025 Cycles and Colourings workshop problems.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02892v1
   The section states Problems 5.1 and 5.2 but neither identifies GP(7,2) nor
   classifies generalized Petersen graphs. The illustrative forbidden fragment
   is a triangular prism with one triangle edge subdivided.

3. Hao, Liu, Romaniello (2026), On 2-Homogeneous Colourings of Cubic Bipartite
   Graphs, arXiv:2611.16649, submitted November 12.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16649
   Readable manuscript mirror: https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2611.16649
   The paper proves the bipartite case and records the claw-free case, leaving
   the general question open. No GP(n,2) classification was found there.

4. Hamed Karami (2026), Perfect 3-colorings of the generalized Petersen graphs
   GP(n,2), arXiv:2611.21435, submitted November 16.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21435
   Readable manuscript mirror: https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2611.21435
   This classifies equitable/perfect three-colorings, not proper
   2-homogeneous colorings. The five surviving matrices have nonzero diagonal
   entries, so their colorings contain adjacent vertices of the same color.
   Its cited older nonexistence of a perfect three-coloring of GP(7,2) is not
   the unrestricted 2-homogeneous nonexistence needed here. The paper contains
   no occurrence of 'fall', 'proper', or 'homogeneous' in the extracted text.

5. Dafik et al. (2017), Several classes of graphs and their r-dynamic chromatic
   numbers, Journal of Physics: Conference Series 855, 012011.
   https://www.researchgate.net/publication/317392540_Several_classes_of_graphs_and_their_r_-dynamic_chromatic_numbers
   DOI: 10.1088/1742-6596/855/1/012011
   A superficially matching theorem says dynamic three-colorability occurs
   exactly when 3 divides n for a graph denoted P_{n,2}. Its definition is the
   PRISM graph, equivalent to GP(n,1), not the generalized Petersen GP(n,2).

TERMINOLOGY CAUTIONS

A dynamic or (k,2)-conditional coloring requires at least two neighbor colors.
With a proper three-color palette that is equivalent to exactly two; with a
four-color palette it permits a rainbow neighborhood and is insufficient here.

A perfect/equitable coloring requires fixed neighbor-color multiplicities for
each vertex-color class. It need not be proper and is not equivalent to
2-homogeneous coloring. A neighborhood-balanced three-coloring is also a
different condition and should not be substituted.

'Petersen coloring' commonly means an edge map to the Petersen graph preserving
edge incidences (Jaeger's problem), which is unrelated to the vertex-coloring
criterion being studied here.

Searches included both homogeneous/homogenous spellings, colour/color forms,
fall coloring, 2-dynamic and conditional coloring, NMNR, role coloring,
independent domination/idomatic partitions, and 'exactly two' neighborhood
phrases combined with generalized Petersen and GP(n,2).

ASSESSMENT

The proposed all-n construction should be documented as independently derived,
with novelty pending. It is outside the already solved bipartite class:
GP(n,2) contains the 5-cycle u_i,u_{i+1},u_{i+2},v_{i+2},v_i,u_i for every
n >= 5. This last observation follows directly from the graph definition.
