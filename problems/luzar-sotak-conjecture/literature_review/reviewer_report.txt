INDEPENDENT REVIEW: TWO-PORT NO-GO AND TEN-TO-SIX VERTEX REPLACEMENT
Date: 2026-11-26

Verdict: the stated local recoloring lemma and exact replacement theorem are
correct under the specified two-pole hypotheses. No counterexample to the
Luzar-Sotak conjecture follows. Instead, the theorem eliminates the previously
proposed ten-vertex fragment from a minimum-order counterexample.

INDEPENDENT CHECKS

review_checks.py uses separately entered literal edges, rather than the previous
graph6 parser or C++ solvers. It verifies all seven displayed extensions of H
and all seven extensions of U = K3,3 minus one edge, with external leaves added.
It also checks every feasible local ordered neighborhood-color configuration
with distinct attachment colors: 96 for a three-color palette, 972 for four
colors, and 5,120 for five colors. Same-colored attachments already repeat a
boundary color and require no recoloring. These finite checks support the
written proof; they are not a substitute for its general argument.

REASON THE LOCAL LEMMA IS SOUND

At an attachment vertex of color P, the two internal neighbors either have
one common color A or have two distinct colors A,B. Properness ensures these
colors differ from P. The permissible color set for its external leaf is
C minus {P,A} in the first case and {A,B} in the second. These sets are exactly
the valid possibilities, not merely sufficient subsets.

With four available colors both sets have size two. For distinct attachment
colors P,Q, either a leaf can match the opposite attachment color, or both
allowed sets are the same two colors outside {P,Q}. In the latter case the
leaves can match each other. With five colors the two sets have size at least
two inside a three-color complement and hence intersect. Only leaf colors
change; their choices are independent when the attachments are distinct.

The six-color local example P=0, Q=1, internal-neighbor colors {2,3} at P and
{4,5} at Q has disjoint allowed sets {2,3} and {4,5}. It shows that this local
intersection argument cannot simply be generalized to arbitrary palettes.
It is not a construction of a global cubic gadget or a counterexample.

BOUNDARY AND CONNECTIVITY EDGE CASES

1. If the two external incidences have the same attachment vertex, the four
   boundary entries already repeat that vertex's color. No independent-leaf
   recoloring argument is needed in this case.
2. If the two distinct attachment vertices are adjacent inside the complement,
   changing the external leaves still changes only the respective attachment
   neighborhoods. No step assumes nonadjacent attachment vertices.
3. The complement may be disconnected. The lemma and extension construction are
   component-independent. Connectivity of the completed graph follows because
   both the old and new fragments are connected and attach to the same incidences.
4. The embedded H must have precisely its displayed ten-vertex internal graph
   and two edges to vertices outside it. Merely containing some of H's edges is
   an insufficient description. If its two free incidences instead join to
   each other, it is a closed ten-vertex component, not the theorem's occurrence.
5. Replacing H by U creates no loop or parallel edge, including when both
   external edges reach the same outside vertex: they reach distinct new ports.
6. The definition should explicitly require each external leaf to have an
   internal neighbor; this excludes a disconnected isolated K2 consisting of
   the two leaves. Every complement arising in the replacement theorem has
   the required property automatically.

PRESERVED PROPERTIES AND LIMITS

Every proper boundary equality pattern has an unrestricted extension in both
fragments. Thus arbitrary colorability of either completed graph is equivalent
to arbitrary colorability of its complementary two-pole. The six-vertex fragment
extends all seven patterns using at most four colors. For the ten-vertex
fragment, the local lemma first makes a boundary repetition, after which its
six corresponding table rows give a four-color extension. Hence the two
completed graphs agree on both arbitrary feasibility and four-color feasibility.

The theorem does not, as stated, prove preservation of three-color feasibility
or the exact minimum number of colors. It also does not prove the general
four-color conjecture. The reduction and local lemma have been derived and
reviewed in this session; priority/novelty has not been established.

TRIANGLE-FREE CAUTION

The archived nonadmissible bridgeless 14-vertex graph with graph6 string
M??OPDDIA_ook?q?? is triangle-free, connected, nonbipartite, and has girth five.
Its unrestricted nonadmissibility is a result of the earlier exact search, not
re-established by the review script. Consequently a proposed unconditional
existence theorem for all triangle-free cubic graphs would conflict with the
existing data. Conditional four-color sufficiency for admissible triangle-free
graphs is a different statement and is not refuted by that observation.

LITERATURE CONTEXT

Hao, Liu and Romaniello (arXiv:2611.16649, 2026), Lemma 3.8, already prove that
splicing edges of two properly 2-homogeneously k-colorable graphs preserves
k-colorability. Their paper also uses prescribed-boundary Heawood three-poles.
Thus the broad methodology of boundary states or edge-splicing is established.
This review does not attribute those existing ideas to the present experiment.
